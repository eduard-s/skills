## Scientific records and claim map

### S1: Cochrane review

[Full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10436683/) · [Official summary](https://www.cochrane.org/evidence/CD013244_blue-light-filtering-spectacle-lenses-visual-performance-macular-back-part-eye-protection-and) · [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37593770/)

Singh et al., *Blue-light filtering spectacle lenses for visual performance, sleep, and macular health in adults*, 18 August 2023, DOI 10.1002/14651858.CD013244.pub2. Eligible PMC/Cochrane paths; full-text relevant sections read, including outcome table, bias, effects, funding and declarations. The direct Cochrane Library full-text route returned 403; PMC browser access succeeded.

- 17 trials/619 people overall; subjective fatigue: three trials/166 people, under one day to five days. Search cutoff: 22 March 2022.
- Fatigue evidence low certainty. Largest trial: mean difference 9.76 symptom-score units, 95% CI -33.95 to 53.47, positive direction worse. Wide uncertainty; no pooled benefit calculated. Some objective-outcome findings differed.
- Supports the brief's limited short-term conclusion and harms-reporting limit; does not establish long-term equivalence or retinal protection.
- Public/institutional funding disclosed. Two review authors investigated an included trial and did not extract or assess bias for that trial themselves.

### S2: Newer positive survey

[Full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12661913/) · [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41322997/)

Akagun, *Long-Term Use of Blue Light-Filtering Glasses and Symptom Improvement in Digital Eye Strain: A Questionnaire-Based Study*, 28 November 2025, Cureus, DOI 10.7759/cureus.98003. Eligible PMC/PubMed locations. Read abstract plus full-text methods, selected results, limitations, conclusions and disclosures through the browser after a blocked web fetch.

- 158/186 (85.0%) recalled improvement in at least one symptom. Cross-sectional survey of lens users; no clear-lens/untreated control. Lens properties unverified; questionnaire lacked formal reliability testing. No financial support/conflicts declared.
- Abstract and appendix refer to at least 12 months, while methods permit at least one month and results include shorter use. Some tabulated symptom denominators also disagree with narrative/group totals. These reporting issues were not resolved with the author; no duration-specific or subgroup effect estimate is used.
- Supports only a report of perceived improvement. Selection, recall and cointerventions prevent attributing it to the filter. It does not overturn a randomized comparison.

### S3: Original trial, overlapping S1

[PubMed abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33587901/)

Singh, Downie and Anderson, *Do Blue-blocking Lenses Reduce Eye Strain From Extended Screen Time? A Double-Masked Randomized Controlled Trial*, June 2021, American Journal of Ophthalmology 226:243–251; online 12 February 2021. DOI 10.1016/j.ajo.2021.02.010. Eligible PubMed record. Web extraction returned a footer; browser reading recovered the complete abstract and metadata. Full paper was not read.

120 symptomatic computer users; two-hour screen task; blue-blocking versus clear placebo lenses with clinician-advocacy randomization. No significant symptom/CFF difference; no adverse events documented in this short task. Funding/conflicts not established from the accessible abstract. Already included in S1: used to explain the experiment, never counted as independent confirmation.
