# Blue-light glasses and screen-related eye strain

Blue-light glasses have not shown a reliable extra benefit over clear lenses for short-term screen-related eye strain. The controlled evidence is limited, so this does not settle whether a particular person might find a pair more comfortable.

The [2023 Cochrane review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10436683/) included 17 randomized trials across several outcomes, but only three assessed subjective visual fatigue. Those three involved 166 people and followed fatigue for less than a day to five days. The review judged this evidence low certainty. Its search ended in March 2022; publication in 2023 does not make it a review of all later work.

One of those trials assigned 120 symptomatic computer users to blue-blocking or clear placebo lenses for a two-hour computer task. It found no significant difference in symptoms. The [trial abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33587901/) helps show what was actually tested; it is already included in Cochrane, so it adds no independent replication here.

A [2025 survey](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12661913/) sounds more encouraging: 158 of 186 respondents, or 85%, reported improvement in at least one symptom. However, respondents were already lens users recalling their experience. There was no clear-lens control group, and other habits could have contributed. This describes reported improvement, rather than measuring how much the filter caused. The paper also gives inconsistent minimum durations of use, weakening its long-term framing.

For a purchase, these findings give little reason to pay extra specifically for a promised eye-strain benefit. Sleep and retinal protection need their own evidence assessment; findings for those outcomes cannot answer this question. Adverse effects were incompletely reported in the review, so the available trials also give a limited account of harms.

**Confidence:** Low certainty about the size of any short-term benefit. The controlled research does not establish a dependable advantage, and the newer survey cannot resolve that uncertainty.

**Scope:** Adults using screens; filtering spectacle lenses compared with clear/nonfiltering lenses where possible. Search completed 1 November 2026, Asia/Dubai. This was a focused evidence check, not a systematic review or a personal diagnosis. Some newer candidate papers could not pass access/publication-status checks.
